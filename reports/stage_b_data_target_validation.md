## 2. Data source and observation period

- File: `<DUONG_DAN_DU_LIEU>/amazon-purchases.csv`, read in place in 250,000-row chunks as text. Reduced in-memory table:
  69.3 MB. The file is never copied or written. A test checks that its size and
  modification time are unchanged after loading.
- MD5 `3549d72735b1211ab009ff0177b94bd9` = official Dataverse v1.0 checksum (doi:10.7910/DVN/YGLYDY).
- **Documentation (verified 2026-10-24 against the article text, PMC11091104).** Berke et al. (2024),
  *Scientific Data* 11, 491, doi:10.1038/s41597-024-03329-6:
  - data were "collected over the period of November 2022 to March 2023";
  - collection stopped on 20 March 2023;
  - participants exported their history "starting from January 1st, 2018 to the current date they were
    completing the study";
  - the authors' own analyses are limited to January 2018 – October 2022.
- **Consequence.** Every respondent's history is fully observed from 2018-01-01 through
  **2022-10-31**. This rests on the documented collection start, not on transaction
  dates: export and survey dates are not in the data. The survey's start *day* in November 2022 is not
  stated; any November day still gives coverage through 31 October 2022.
- **Transaction dates.** 2018-01-01 to 2024-08-15. Every one of the
  1,765 calendar days from 2018-01-01 to
  2022-10-31 has transactions (minimum 417, median
  1055 rows per day), so there is no gap inside the study period.
  29 rows from 9 respondents are dated
  after 20 March 2023. They lie outside every window and are kept but flagged.

**Policies.**
- **Missing text fields.** Rows are kept. Missing Title or Category does not make a purchase ineligible.
- **Exact duplicates.** Kept: they may be genuine repeat purchases. They cannot change any label, because the
  target uses distinct purchase days and identical rows share a date. This is confirmed empirically: the
  dedup panel is identical to the raw panel (True). Duplicate detection uses a
  128-bit key from two independent 64-bit hashes of the raw text. The independent check (SHA-1) gives the
  same count.
- **Gift cards.** Kept in the primary analysis, excluded in the sensitivity analysis (§6). Our rule flags
  41,483 rows from 3,128 respondents; the descriptor reports 40,368 rows
  from 3,220 users with a title-keyword rule that is not fully published. The difference cannot be
  reconciled from the documentation (**unresolved**; it affects only the sensitivity version).
- **Survey.** Only the ID column of `survey.csv` is read, to reconcile identifiers. No survey variable enters
  the panel: the panel's columns are fixed and tested, and `assert_no_survey_columns` is available for Stage C.

## 4. Cutoffs and target definition

- **Cutoffs:** 15 quarter-ends, 2018-12-31 … 2022-06-30. Each has ≥ 12 months of history, and its window ends
  ≤ 2022-10-31. Latest admissible cutoff: 2022-08-02. The
  2022-09-30 cutoff (window to 2022-12-29) receives **no label**; a test asserts it raises instead of
  producing negatives.
- **History at c:** purchase days with 2018-01-01 ≤ day ≤ c (the cutoff day belongs to the history).
- **Eligible at c:** ≥ 1 purchase day in the history. Nothing after c is used.
- **Target window:** days c+1 … c+90 inclusive (90 calendar days). **y = 1** if ≥ 1 eligible purchase day
  falls in the window, otherwise 0.
- **Eligible purchase (primary):** any row of the file in the window, including gift cards, duplicates and
  rows with missing text fields.
- **Gaps between windows.** Consecutive windows never overlap. Because quarters have 90–92 days, these
  17 days belong to no window:
  2019-06-30, 2019-09-29, 2019-09-30, 2019-12-30, 2019-12-31, 2020-03-31, 2020-06-30, 2020-09-29, 2020-09-30, 2020-12-30, 2020-12-31, 2021-06-30, 2021-09-29, 2021-09-30, 2021-12-30, 2021-12-31, 2022-06-30. They still enter the next cutoff's history.

## 11. Unresolved limitations

1. **Unit.** `Survey ResponseID` is a random survey identifier (descriptor, Data Records), not a verified
   Amazon customer ID. Accounts may be shared among household members, and respondents may also buy through
   other accounts or retailers. Results describe purchases recorded in this export.
2. **Not churn.** y = 0 means no recorded purchase day in 90 days. Most such respondents buy again (§8).
3. **Coverage assumption.** Full coverage through 2022-10-31 rests on the documented collection start
   (November 2022) and export rule. Per-respondent export dates are not in the data and cannot be checked.
   778 respondents have no purchase after
   2022-10-31. Under the documentation this is inactivity; an early, undocumented export would look the
   same.
4. **Panel selection.** Respondents were recruited in 2022–23. Being in the panel conditions on having an
   Amazon account and being willing to share data at that time, which may favour returners at early cutoffs.
   This cannot be corrected; findings describe this panel.
5. **Left truncation.** Histories start on 2018-01-01. 3,351
   respondents already buy in 2018 Q1, so "first purchase" is the first *observed* purchase.
6. **`Order Date` semantics.** The descriptor lists the column but does not define it (order placement vs.
   shipment). We treat it as the purchase date.
7. **Gift-card rule.** It is not identical to the descriptor's (§3).
8. **Exploratory history.** The data, cutoffs and related horizons were explored in the rfm_v3 thesis. The
   90-day binary target is new, but it is not independent of that exploration. Stage D settings should be
   frozen before any model result is seen.
9. **Duplicate detection** is hash-based (128-bit). The count agrees with an independent SHA-1 check, but
   individual flagged pairs were not compared field by field.
